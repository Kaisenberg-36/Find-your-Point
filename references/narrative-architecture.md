## Input and responsibility

Preserve Gate 0's current intent and audience assumptions, Gate 1's evidence-linked
units and limitations, and Gate 2's cognitive direction, Primary/Local reasoning
and intended endpoint. Understanding-only remains a complete endpoint.

Gate 2 determines required cognitive transitions. Gate 3 determines how to unfold
those transitions with understandable sequence, focus, returns and synthesis.
A transition may need several scenes; several simple transitions may share one.
If the spine needs substantive revision, surface the affected upstream issue rather
than silently changing the objective. Do not restart earlier Gates for routine detail.

## Units defined by cognitive job

| Unit | Responsibility | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Act | A substantial cognitive phase: what important understanding differs at its end? | Optional grouping; no required three-act arc or one Act per Primary Pattern. |
| Scene | A sufficiently complete local cognitive task: establish context, explain a mechanism, connect evidence, compare, synthesize or establish a limitation. | Not a topic heading or mandatory one-to-one mapping from spine nodes or Local Patterns. |
| State | The smallest useful cognitive rhythm unit: what should the viewer notice or understand now? | Not a slide, animation instruction, or Gate 0 machine-state object. Split only when attention or a dependency requires it. |

Use the lightest structure that explains the path. Do not create Act/Scene/State
containers merely for symmetry. A Local Pattern can span scenes, or a scene can
resolve a small local logic. Patterns are grammars, not structural levels.
A State's future expression could be static, revealed or revisited; do not decide
that here. It is also distinct from the later motion requirement of a Stable State.

## Constructing the path

1. Start at the audience's evidenced or explicitly assumed entry knowledge and the
   authorized endpoint. Identify the minimum dependencies between understandings.
2. Group related dependencies into scenes with a cognitive job and exit understanding.
   Explain why each important scene occurs here: what the viewer already knows,
   what is added, and what breaks or becomes harder if it is removed or moved.
   If nothing useful changes, merge, omit or mark it optional. Multiple sequences
   can be valid; cognitive necessity is a reason for placement, not a unique order.
3. Within a scene, separate States when the viewer must first distinguish something
   before combining it, shift attention to a new question, or pause on a conclusion
   and its limit. Merge distinctions that can be understood together. Do not split
   every fact into a State or equate elapsed time with cognitive progress.
4. For each important transition, name what carries forward and why the next step
   starts here. Keep qualifiers attached to the claims they constrain.
5. Plan where viewers focus, where they synthesize, and which earlier information
   must return. Stop at the authorized endpoint; no automatic Next Steps scene.

A compact working note can capture entry understanding → cognitive job → evidence
and limits → exit understanding → reason for the next step, plus Focus/Synthesis
and any return needed. Use existing claim IDs/locators, not a duplicate ledger.
This is a thinking aid, not a required user worksheet or fixed serialization.

## Continuity and memory

Continuity is a meaningful relationship the viewer can follow, not merely smooth
wording. It can carry a question, evidence, object/concept, perspective, structure
or logical dependency. Name the actual relationship; do not force all six types.

Chronological adjacency is not causation. Parallel observations remain parallel;
a new perspective does not establish a trend. When a link requires absent evidence,
retain the gap, take a supported route or stop that branch. Never fill it with an
invented outcome, capability, audience belief or proposal presented as fact.

Reduce the viewer's need to search, remember, match, compare or infer unaided:

- **Evidence return:** reintroduce the specific earlier evidence needed now, with
  its original scope and caveat; avoid retelling all background.
- **Concept return:** restate the distinction whose meaning the next step depends on.
- **Persistent anchor:** keep one conceptual question or reference object available
  throughout a passage. This specifies narrative need, not a screen element.
- **Synthesis:** reconnect established parts and explain their relationship explicitly.

Focus isolates one necessary distinction; Synthesis restores its place in the whole.
An orienting synthesis may precede focus when context is needed. Focus-first is not
a rule. Avoid both an initial information dump and isolated details with no reunion.
No universal number of States, duration or memory threshold is prescribed.

## One tiny synthetic illustration

Reuse the prior synthetic understanding-only task: explain why recorded inventory
is not a dispatch promise. The established spine distinguishes on-hand, available,
authorized and dispatched. Invent no new business evidence or audience attitudes.

One optional Act: understand the boundary between inventory information and supply.

- **Scene 1 — interpret the quantity.** Entry: viewer has the inventory figure.
  State A focuses on the existing record: 12 on hand, including 8 reserved and 2
  awaiting inspection. State B connects these categories to 2 available. Its job
  is the number's meaning, not evaluating stock policy. Removing this scene would
  leave the next permission discussion disconnected from availability.
- **Scene 2 — interpret the promise.** Return to the same 2 available items rather
  than requiring recall. State A focuses on the existing rule requiring the owning
  region's confirmation and the record confirming 2 items. State B synthesizes:
  the record supports availability and confirmation, but has no dispatch evidence.
  Multiple spine distinctions fit this scene. The shared object and rule explain
  why it follows Scene 1; no assertion that confirmation caused shipment is made.

Endpoint: viewer can explain what the inventory record does and does not establish.
No reform recommendation or dispatch success is added. These are illustrative
cognitive States, not a two-slide plan, visual storyboard or new behavioral Trial.

## Lightweight Viewing Path Audit

At important nodes ask:

1. What should the viewer recognize now?
2. What prior information must they remember, and should it return here?
3. What understanding does this step add?
4. Why does the next step follow?
5. Is this Focus or Synthesis, or is a split useful?
6. Does the connection introduce an unsupported implication?

Use these questions to adjust the path, not score it or generate an audit report.
Minimal completion check: starts from the spine; explains sequence; retains evidence
boundaries; avoids a mechanical hierarchy; no blocker likely to cause major rework.
Then stop. No regression suite, independent judge or schema engineering is implied.

Still flexible: grouping, State granularity, return frequency and handoff format.
Candidate to examine in a later Trial: whether deletion/reordering checks help reveal
unnecessary scenes and memory burdens without over-fragmenting an explanation.
